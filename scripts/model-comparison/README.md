# Model comparison for AI search — baseline and toolkit

Two things live here: the **2026-09-18 baseline** comparing `claude-opus-5` and
`claude-sonnet-5` across adaptive-thinking effort levels, and the **tools that
produced it**, kept reusable so the next model or the next prompt edit can be
measured against this same evidence rather than judged from scratch. Start at
[Findings](#findings) for what was learned; jump to [Tools](#tools) and
[Running a later comparison](#running-a-later-comparison) to add to it.

Every run goes through the real AI search path (`lib/ai-search.ts`) against the
**real dinner database** — the same snapshot the Tonight screen would send.

The baseline was generated by `scripts/ai-search-eval.ts --compare` on
2026-09-18, against an anchor day of 2026-09-17 (Thursday): 35 Options, 175
logged dinners, a 27,579-token cached snapshot prefix.

> **The baseline predates the Log-source fix (architecture-deepening issue
> 06).** Until then, `buildSnapshotFromDb` in `scripts/ai-search-eval.ts` and
> `scripts/model-comparison/show-prompt.mjs` both fed the snapshot
> `getTonightData(today).logEntries` — the deterministic ranking's **non-future**
> Log — instead of production's `getFullLogForSnapshot()`, which also carries
> future-dated (Planned) dinners (ADR-0008). Every run above, and every metric
> derived from it, was measured against that narrower Log; it is not
> invalidated, but it is not directly comparable to a sweep run after the fix,
> since the Log a model reasons over changed. Do not silently re-baseline —
> note it if you run a fresh sweep and want to compare against the numbers
> here.

> **The reason-quality metrics changed on 2026-09-19.** The system prompt was
> reworked to stop rationales reading formulaically, and `analyze.mjs` was
> re-pointed at the new rules — so the reason-quality numbers in `summary.md`
> are NOT comparable with any copy taken before that date. Three things moved:
> the single `pattern vocab` share split into `rhythm` (naming a habit's shape
> — good) and `vague tic` (the departure-from-rotation family — capped at one
> per response), because the old combined number scored "dropped out of
> rotation" as *success*; `dateRefs` widened from precise elapsed time to
> elapsed time in any form, vague duration included; and `compound` dropped
> from violation to informational. A new `opens` column counts distinct opening
> words. The runs themselves are unchanged and were re-scored in place.
>
> Three metric details were tuned while scoring the first post-change sweep,
> all in the direction of measuring the stated rule and nothing wider:
> `withoutIntervals` strips cadence phrasings before the elapsed-time test, so
> "runs about every two weeks" — the prompt's own model answer — no longer
> scores as a violation; `VAGUE_VOCAB` is scoped to the family the prompt
> actually caps, after a wider version reported 4.3 uses on a run whose real
> capped-family count was 1.0; and the reason-quality tables are keyed by
> snapshot as well as cell, so a prompt change's runs cannot be averaged in
> with the runs that motivated it.
>
> **Findings 1-12 below are unaffected** — they compare models and effort
> levels on latency, cost, stability and ordering, none of which these metrics
> feed. Only the reason-quality tables in `summary.md` moved. The one caveat is
> finding 3, which quotes rationales as evidence of habit reasoning: those
> quotes are still accurate about what the *old* prompt produced, and the
> phrasings it praises ("has fallen out of rotation") are exactly what the new
> prompt suppresses. The finding — that effort, not model, buys habit reasoning
> — stands; its example phrasings are now historical.

## Findings

**1. Sonnet 5 at `effort: high` does not fit the app's latency budget.** On the
open query it took **217.3s** — 2.4× the 90s `REQUEST_TIMEOUT_MS` — and 3/3
calls under the production budget aborted, which the fail-safe path collapses to
`AI_SEARCH_UNAVAILABLE` (the household silently gets the deterministic recency
list). The narrowing query is *marginal rather than hopeless*: 90s+ on one
attempt, 59.5s on another. Its successful runs are probes taken with
`AI_TIMEOUT_MS=300000` so the call could finish and be judged at all — they
carry `source: "sonnet5-high-open.json"` / `"sonnet5-high-narrowing.json"`,
while the two `ok: false` runs beside them are the same cell under the real
90s budget.

**2. Sonnet 5 at `effort: low` is unstable on byte-identical input.** Two reps
over one shared snapshot shared only **1 of 5** top-5 Options and disagreed on
#1. Compare `opus-5 · high` at 5/5 and `opus-5 · low` at 3/5, both with a
stable #1. For a dinner picker this is the most damaging result in the set: the
household gets a materially different answer on every search of unchanged data.

**3. Effort, not model, is what buys habit reasoning.** Both models at `high`
name genuine cadence and day-of-week patterns — the thing ADR-0005 exists for
("Runs on a roughly two-week cadence and that stretch is now up", "A recurring
Thursday choice", "follows a roughly monthly rhythm"). Both at `low` largely
paraphrase recency ("hasn't come up in over a month"), which is close to
duplicating the deterministic ranking the AI path is supposed to beat.

**4. Sonnet 5 makes content errors Opus 5 does not.** At `low` it ranked Aji
Ichi — a *Japanese* restaurant — #1 on the open query with the rationale
"Mediterranean slot is due since Mazra just filled that recently": the reason
does not match the Option and is self-contradictory. On the narrowing query it
ranked Hungryroot #2 as "overdue" despite the standing household note "not in
the house this week"; `opus-5 · high` demoted it and said exactly that. At
`high` it spread a single "swim-day pick" pattern across three unrelated
Options.

**5. The Thursday trap.** Tadu carries "no Thursday dinner service" and the
anchor day is a Thursday. `opus-5 · high` buried it at #34 and `sonnet-5 · high`
at #31 — both correct. Both `low` cells put it at #11, Sonnet citing an
unrelated reason ("standing disappointment from last visit").

**6. Sonnet 5 is not the cheap option here.** It generates 3.5-3.9× the output
tokens, which eats and then reverses its 2.5× cheaper rate once the snapshot
prefix is cached:

| cell | out tok | cold-cache $/call | warm-cache $/call |
|---|---|---|---|
| `opus-5 · low` | 1,416 | $0.208 | **$0.049** |
| `sonnet-5 · low` | 4,905 | $0.118 | $0.055 |
| `opus-5 · high` | 6,499 | $0.335 | $0.176 |
| `sonnet-5 · high` | 25,297 | $0.322 | $0.259 |

At $5/$25 vs $2/$10 per MTok, with a 27,579-token prefix at 1.25× (write) or
0.1× (read). Cold, Sonnet 5 low is genuinely cheaper — input dominates. Warm —
the real steady state, since the snapshot is sent in a `cache_control` block
precisely so a burst of searches reuses it — the two `low` cells are at parity
and `sonnet-5 · high` is the most expensive cell measured. Caches are
model-scoped, so a model switch also forfeits the warm prefix.

**7. The Opus effort levels, at 4-5 reps each.** Cost and latency scale almost
perfectly linearly — each step roughly doubles both — while stability does not
track them. Measured over every rep on the shared Thursday snapshot:

| cell | latency | out tok | n | mean top-5 overlap | #1 held | warm $/call |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `opus-5 · low` | 21.4s | ~1,450 | 5 | 2.9/5 | **5/5** | $0.050 |
| `opus-5 · medium` | 44.5s | ~3,400 | 5 | 2.3/5 | 4/5 | $0.099 |
| `opus-5 · high` | 78.9s | ~6,470 | 4 | **4.0/5** | 3/4 | $0.176 |

`high` reproduces its top 5 far better than either lower level (4.0/5 against
2.3-2.9), but it is *not* the most reliable on the single pick the household
actually acts on: `low` returned Taqueria Durango at #1 in all five reps, while
`high` slipped on one of four. The two axes disagree, and which matters depends
on whether the Tonight screen is read as "the pick" or "the shortlist".

`medium` remains hard to justify: 2× low's cost and latency for a *lower* mean
overlap and a less reliable #1. It does do real habit reasoning — it is the
only cell that visibly read the **future-dated** Log ("passed tomorrow for the
same fried rice reason", ADR-0008 working) — but it is also the worst offender
on the no-calendar-dates rule ("since mid-June", "since spring", "quiet since
July") and writes the compound two-clause rationales the prompt forbids.

**Correction from the first pass.** At 2 reps, `medium` measured 1/5 overlap
with a different #1, and `high` measured a perfect 5/5 with a stable #1 — which
read as "medium is dramatically the worst". Neither survived more data:
`medium` is 2.3/5 with #1 held 4/5, and `high` is 4.0/5 with #1 held 3/4. The
original numbers were sample noise at n=2, exactly as flagged at the time. The
conclusion that survived is the weaker one — medium buys nothing over low — and
the ranking of `low` vs `medium` by overlap (2.9 vs 2.3) is still close enough
at n=5 that it should not be leaned on.

**8. Effort only matters on the open query.** On `"something light"` all three
Opus levels return essentially the same shortlist — Little Vietnamese Bistro,
Roma Deli, Chicken and Rice, Mollie Stone's, Tomato Salad — at 7.6s / 12.9s /
20.9s. When the query does the narrowing, paying for thinking buys nothing.
Effort earns its cost only where habit inference is the whole task.

**9. On whole-list ordering — the axis that matters for scanning — the Opus
effort levels are nearly tied, and Sonnet 5 is the clear outlier.** Top-5
overlap only answers "is the headline pick stable". Someone reading down the
list for inspiration cares whether the *ordering* holds together, measured by
Spearman over every shared Option and by how far the average Option moves
between runs:

| cell | n | Spearman | mean move | overlap@5 | overlap@10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `opus-5 · high` | 4 | 0.78 | 5.0 places | 0.80 | 0.57 |
| `opus-5 · low` | 5 | 0.76 | 4.9 places | 0.58 | 0.64 |
| `opus-5 · medium` | 5 | 0.70 | 5.4 places | 0.46 | 0.56 |
| `sonnet-5 · low` | 6 | **0.42** | **7.6 places** | 0.45 | 0.43 |

Chance baseline on a 35-Option list: overlap@5 ≈ 0.14, @10 ≈ 0.29, @20 ≈ 0.57.

Two things follow. **The three Opus levels cluster at 0.70-0.78** — effort
barely moves whole-list *self*-consistency, so `high`'s advantage is much
narrower on this axis than its 4.0/5 top-5 overlap suggested. **Sonnet 5 low
sits well apart at 0.42**, with the average Option shifting nearly 8 places
between runs against ~5 for every Opus level. At n=6 this is the firmest
version of the instability finding, and it is measured on the scan-the-list
axis rather than the headline pick.

_(These four numbers moved by ≤0.02 when `analyze.mjs` was made reusable:
Spearman now renumbers ranks within the set both lists returned, which is the
only form the statistic is defined for when two lists differ in depth. The
earlier figures were 0.78 / 0.74 / 0.69 / 0.40. Nothing about the ranking
changed — the arithmetic was corrected, and the correction matters far more for
narrowing-query shortlists, where the old form produced values as absurd as
-30.5.)_

**10. Sonnet 5's reasons read better but are less trustworthy and worse
tiered.** Per-cell means over the open query:

| cell | reps | pattern vocab | compound | len gradient | empty at rank | conflicts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `opus-5 · high` | 4 | 33% | 17% | -0.84 | 0.94 | 0 |
| `opus-5 · low` | 5 | 29% | 27% | -0.88 | 0.89 | 0 |
| `opus-5 · medium` | 5 | 34% | **36%** | -0.82 | 0.88 | 0 |
| `sonnet-5 · low` | 6 | **62%** | **8%** | **-0.63** | **0.69** | **2** |

Sonnet 5 reaches for habit language twice as often as any Opus cell (62% vs
29-34%) and breaks the one-clause rule far *less* (8% vs 17-36%). Line by line
its rationales genuinely read better — terser, more pattern-shaped. Against
that:

- **It blanks reasons mid-list.** `empty at rank` 0.69 means Sonnet's empty
  rationales average 69% of the way down, where Opus concentrates them at
  0.88-0.94 — at the very bottom, which is the tiering `pithy` mode asks for.
  Scanning a Sonnet list, you hit unexplained gaps partway down; scanning an
  Opus list, the blanks are all below the point you stopped caring.
- **Its length gradient is the shallowest** (-0.63 vs -0.82 to -0.88), the same
  failure from the other direction: reason length tracks how weak the pick is
  less reliably.
- **Both cuisine conflicts in the entire set are Sonnet's, and they reproduce.**
  Aji Ichi — a Japanese restaurant — came back at **#1** with a Mediterranean
  rationale in two independent runs ("Mediterranean slot is due since Mazra just
  filled that recently"; "the standing pick when Mediterranean rotation comes
  due"). The fluent habit vocabulary is sometimes attached to the wrong Option
  entirely, which is worse than a blunt rationale: it is confidently wrong.

`opus-5 · medium` is the worst compound-clause offender (36%), consistent with
finding 7.

**11. Sonnet 5 low's #1 is bimodal, and both modes are Options Opus buries.**
Across the 6 reps the top pick is Aji Ichi three times and Mazra three times —
not drift around a consensus, but two distinct answers each returned half the
time. Both are Options every Opus cell treats as too recent to pick: Opus ranks
Aji Ichi around #31 (sushi twice in the week, Ninja Sushi just eaten) and Mazra
#7-#33 ("often a Thursday pick, but its usual gap hasn't passed yet"). So the
disagreement is not a matter of taste at the margin — Sonnet's confident top
pick is, in both modes, something the household plausibly just ate. Big Mouth
Burgers is the one genuinely stable element, appearing in all 6 top-5s.

**12. `low` and `high` are equally self-consistent but do not produce the same
list.** Finding 9 measures each cell against *itself*. Measuring cells against
each other — every rep of A against every rep of B, on the shared snapshot —
separates "is it stable" from "is it the same answer":

| cell A | cell B | pairs | Spearman | mean move | vs each cell's own noise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `opus-5 · high` | `opus-5 · medium` | 20 | 0.72 | 5.4 places | **indistinguishable** (floor 0.70) |
| `opus-5 · low` | `opus-5 · medium` | 25 | 0.69 | 5.6 places | level with it (floor 0.69) |
| `opus-5 · high` | `opus-5 · low` | 20 | **0.58** | 6.6 places | **0.17 below** (floor 0.76) |
| `opus-5 · low` | `sonnet-5 · low` | 30 | 0.41 | 7.6 places | 0.02 below (floor 0.42) |
| `opus-5 · high` | `sonnet-5 · low` | 24 | 0.34 | 8.7 places | 0.08 below (floor 0.42) |

The floor is the weaker of the two cells' own rep-to-rep Spearman: a pair at or
above it orders the list no more differently than one of the cells differs from
*itself*, so any difference between them is not yet evidence of anything.

**`high` vs `low` at 0.58 clears that bar** — it is the one Opus pair that
genuinely disagrees, with the average Option landing 6.6 places apart against
~5 within either cell. So "the effort levels are nearly tied" (finding 9) is a
statement about *stability*, not about agreement: `low` and `high` are each
about as self-consistent as the other while returning materially different
orderings. Choosing between them is choosing which list, not just how much to
pay for the same one. `medium`, by contrast, is indistinguishable from `high`
and level with `low` — it holds no ordering of its own, which is the
ordering-side version of finding 7.

**Read of it:** `opus-5 · low` (today's production setting) is the right default
— cheapest, fastest, and the most reliable on the #1 pick. `opus-5 · high` is
the one upgrade worth considering, and only if the *shortlist* matters more than
the top pick: it holds its top 5 together (4.0/5) and is the most consistently
pattern-driven, for 3.5× the warm cost and ~4× the latency. `medium` is the
setting to skip. Sonnet 5 has no winning cell: `low` is unstable (2.3/5 overlap
at n=6, #1 held 3/6 and bimodal — finding 11) and barely cheaper warm, `high`
blows the 90s timeout and costs more than Opus 5 high. If latency ever binds,
cut effort rather than switch models.

For the scan-down-the-list use paradigm specifically, the case for `high` is
weaker than the top-5 numbers imply: whole-list Spearman is 0.78 vs `low`'s
0.76, a gap inside the noise at these sample sizes. What `high` does buy is a
tighter top 5 (4.0/5 vs 2.9/5), fewer compound rationales (17% vs 27%), and
blanks pushed furthest to the bottom (0.94). If the household reads the top
handful and stops, that is worth something; if they scan the whole list, `low`
is about as self-consistent for a third of the cost — though per finding 12 it
is not the *same* list, so this is a choice between two orderings rather than a
discount on one.

`sonnet-5 · low` is now n=6 and its instability holds up — Spearman 0.42 and
7.6 places of drift, against 0.70-0.78 and ~5 for every Opus cell.
`sonnet-5 · high` remains n=1 (it aborts under the production timeout), so its
one strong-looking run should not be read as a trend.

**13. The rationale rework moved every style measure, at no latency or token
cost.** `opus-5 · low`, the production cell, before (2026-09-17 anchor, n=5)
against after (2026-09-18 anchor, n=3):

| measure | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| rhythm — names a habit's shape | 13% | **29%** |
| vague tic — capped at 1/response | 3.4 | **1.0** |
| opens — distinct opening words | 78% | **95%** |
| repeated — verbatim duplicate reasons | 3.8 | **0.3** |
| compound — two-clause reasons | 27% | 58% |
| latency | 21.4s | 19.3s |
| output tokens | ~1,450 | ~1,200 |

The compound rise is intended, not a regression: pairing a habit with a note
needs two clauses, and the ban that suppressed it was lifted deliberately. The
capped tic landed exactly on its cap. What the numbers do not show is the
qualitative shift — the model now reaches for Log notes and household members
by name ("Vietnamese runs roughly monthly and that turn has arrived, with rice
for Sonia and fried rice for Helen") and for stale Rejections ("Passed over
Monday only because pasta was too fresh — that reason has expired"), neither of
which appeared above the tail before.

Two caveats. The two sweeps are on **different anchor days**, so this is a
style comparison, not a ranking one — the tables are keyed by snapshot for
exactly that reason, and nothing here says the *ordering* improved. And a new
tic may be forming: "rare" ran to 8 uses across the 3 reps, including two
near-identical "Rarely chosen" rows. It is outside the family the prompt caps,
so `vague tic` does not see it; `opens` and `repeated` would catch it if it
spreads.

**14. Opus 5.5 replaced Opus 5 as the production model, at `effort: low`.**
`claude-opus-5-5` on the 2026-09-22 anchor (Tuesday, 35 Options, 185 Log
entries), open query only — `2026-09-22-open.json`:

| cell | n | latency | out tok | Spearman | top-5 overlap | #1 held | vague tic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `opus-5-5 · low` | 5 | **13.8s** | ~1,210 | **0.84** | **3.9/5** | 3/5 | **0.0** |
| `opus-5-5 · medium` | 4 | 19.4s | ~1,910 | 0.51 | 2.7/5 | 2/4 | 0.3 |

Against Opus 5 (finding 7 and 13: `low` 19-21s, Spearman 0.76, overlap 2.9/5,
vague tic 1.0 on the reworked prompt; `medium` 44.5s) it is faster at both
levels and breaks the capped-vocabulary rule less. The comparison crosses
**anchor days** — `summary.md` refuses to pair the cells for that reason — so
it is a latency-and-style read, not an ordering one.

`low` over `medium`: `medium` is the less stable of the two, and the cause is
specific. Thai Nakorn, Little Vietnamese Bistro and Taqueria Durango were
already Picked for the anchor day — `medium` put them at #1-3 in reps 1 and 4
and at #32-34 in the other two, while every `low` rep buried them. The
instability was the model disagreeing with itself about what an already-Picked
Option means, not about the Catalog. That motivated dropping anchor-day Picked
Options from the candidates (`buildSnapshot`); `medium` has not been re-run
since, so whether it steadies without them is open.

**15. Opus 5.5 leaves out the one Option with nothing to say about it.** Sofra
Grill has no Log history, notes or Rejections, and it is missing from all 10
Opus 5.5 rankings — 9 in `2026-09-22-open.json` plus the one in
`2026-09-22-open-picked-out.json`. The screen shows only returned rows in AI
view, so it silently vanished. Two fixes followed that run: a prompt line
requiring every candidate (with a never-tried Option allowed to rank high for
novelty), and `search` appending any candidate still omitted on an open query,
marked "Not ranked — the AI left this one out" rather than left blank (a blank
reads as the model's "obviously bad pick" in `pithy` mode).

**The prompt line did not work; the append did.** One `opus-5-5 · low` run on
the final prompt (`2026-09-22-open-every-candidate.json`, n=1): its `rawText`
holds 31 ranked lines and still no Sofra Grill — 11 of 11 runs now — and
`backfilledCount` is 1, the append putting it back at #32. The same run's
first line was `OPEN`, the marker `search` reads to treat a non-empty
non-narrowing query as open, so that half of the contract is followed. The
every-candidate line is cheap and kept, but the append is what the screen
relies on.

## Fireworks exploration (2026-09-22, not part of the baseline)

A separate, one-off look at whether a non-Anthropic model on Fireworks could
serve AI search — prompted by the `kimi-search` branch name. These three runs
are single reps (n=1 each) against **today's live snapshot** (2026-09-22,
Tuesday), not the 2026-09-17 baseline anchor, so none of it pools with
Findings 1-13 or `summary.md` — it is a rough first look, not a measurement.

Fireworks exposes an Anthropic-compatible `/v1/messages` endpoint, so
`createAiSearchClient` reaches it with no code change beyond a `.env` swap:
`ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` points at `https://api.fireworks.ai/inference` and
`ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is a Fireworks key. `scripts/ai-search-eval.ts` carries one
fixed `--compare` cell (`FIREWORKS_MODEL`, currently `gpt-oss-120b`) for
whichever Fireworks model is under test, always at `thinking: off` — Fireworks'
compat layer does not support Anthropic's `thinking: {type: "adaptive"}` (the
scheme Opus 5 / Sonnet 5's effort levels use), only `off` or `enabled` +
its own `reasoning_effort`, which the harness does not wire up yet. Run with
`--cells="fireworks"` so only that cell fires — the same `.env` would also
misroute every Anthropic cell to Fireworks if the matrix ran unfiltered.

| Model | Fireworks id | Result | Latency | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kimi K3 | `kimi-k3` | **UNAVAILABLE** | 90.0s (timeout) | Zero output before the production 90s abort. Fireworks' largest/priciest Kimi variant (1M context, $3/$15 per M) despite being labeled "Instant" |
| Kimi K2.6 | `kimi-k2p6` | **UNAVAILABLE** | 90.0s (timeout) | Same outcome as K3, on a much smaller/cheaper model (262K context, $0.95/$4 per M) — the timeout is not K3-specific |
| gpt-oss-120b | `gpt-oss-120b` | ok | 44.9s | 35/35 Options ranked, 22,185 in / 4,967 out tokens |

Both Kimi calls burned the full 90s timeout with no partial output, which
matches Fireworks' own documented guidance that the Kimi K2 family "can be
slow on large inputs" and recommends 10-30 *minute* client timeouts for
multi-step agent calls — this app's 90s budget is not in the range Fireworks
designs Kimi's latency around. Neither call was retried with
`AI_TIMEOUT_MS` raised, so it is unknown whether either would eventually
succeed; only that neither fits production as configured.

`gpt-oss-120b` completed comfortably inside budget, but its one rationale
sample reads weaker than the Opus baseline on the two axes Findings 1-13
care about most: most rows open with the Option's own name (the prompt
explicitly forbids this, and `opus-5` rarely does it), most reasons restate
recency ("hasn't appeared in weeks", "last visited July") rather than naming
a cadence or day-of-week pattern, and the tail does not shrink toward empty
the way `pithy` mode asks — the bottom of the list is still full sentences.
That profile reads closer to `sonnet-5 · low` in Findings 3/4/10 than to any
Opus cell. One rep is far below the n=3 floor Findings 1-13 hold to, so this
is a lead, not a conclusion — a real read would need several reps on a shared
anchor day, exactly as `analyze.mjs` expects, plus a pass through
`ground-truth.mjs` before trusting any of its stated household constraints.

The three run files (`kimi-test.json`, `kimi-k2p6-test.json`,
`gpt-oss-120b-test.json`) are single-cell, single-rep documents in the same
shape `--compare --json=` always writes — `analyze.mjs` and `where-is.mjs`
can read them, but pooling them against the baseline files would violate the
snapshot-identity rule `runs.mjs` enforces (different anchor day, different
provider, no shared `cache_control` prefix), so treat them as three
standalone data points rather than a fourth baseline file.

## The cells

A **cell** is one model at one thinking setting, named by the label
`scripts/ai-search-eval.ts` gives it. The baseline measures five:

| Label | Model | Thinking |
|---|---|---|
| `opus-5 · effort low` | `claude-opus-5` | `{type: "adaptive"}` + `output_config.effort: low` |
| `opus-5 · effort medium` | `claude-opus-5` | `{type: "adaptive"}` + `output_config.effort: medium` |
| `opus-5 · effort high` | `claude-opus-5` | `{type: "adaptive"}` + `output_config.effort: high` |
| `sonnet-5 · effort low` | `claude-sonnet-5` | `{type: "adaptive"}` + `output_config.effort: low` |
| `sonnet-5 · effort high` | `claude-sonnet-5` | `{type: "adaptive"}` + `output_config.effort: high` |

Finding 14 added two more, on its own anchor day:

| Label | Model | Thinking |
|---|---|---|
| `opus-5-5 · effort low` | `claude-opus-5-5` | `{type: "adaptive"}` + `output_config.effort: low` |
| `opus-5-5 · effort medium` | `claude-opus-5-5` | `{type: "adaptive"}` + `output_config.effort: medium` |

`opus-5-5 · effort low` is the app's production setting (`MODEL_DEFAULT` and
`EFFORT_DEFAULT` in `lib/ai-search.ts`).
Both models take effort through the same `output_config.effort` knob, so the
cells are comparable level-for-level.

The full matrix comes from `ADAPTIVE_MODELS` × `ADAPTIVE_EFFORTS` in
`scripts/ai-search-eval.ts` — add a model to that list and it becomes a cell.
Note the API-family split in `lib/ai-search.ts`: `budget_tokens` is rejected
with a 400 by Opus 5 / Sonnet 5 and is only sent to the models named in
`BUDGET_API_MODELS` (Sonnet 4.6, Haiku 4.5). A new model lands on the adaptive
path by default, which is correct; a new *legacy* model has to be added to that
list or every one of its calls fails — and the fail-safe path collapses the
failure to `AI_SEARCH_UNAVAILABLE`, so it looks like a slow model, not a broken
request. Check a new cell's first run really succeeded (`ok` in the JSON)
before reading anything into its numbers.

## The two queries

Both queries run over **one shared snapshot per run** — every cell in a given
run sees byte-identical Catalog / Log / Rejections data, so a ranking difference
between cells is the model, not the input.

- **`2026-09-17-open.json`** — empty query, 4-6 reps per cell. The hard case:
  with no query to narrow on, the prompt's entire task is reading eating
  *habits* out of the dated Log (cadence, day-of-week rhythm, streaks, drift)
  and ranking the whole candidate Catalog by them. Several reps so run-to-run
  nondeterminism can be told apart from a real model difference.
- **`2026-09-17-something-light.json`** — the query `"something light"`, 1 rep
  per cell. Tests instruction adherence rather than habit inference: the prompt
  says a genuinely narrowing query must return a focused shortlist of fitting
  Options, **not** the whole Catalog re-sorted.

## Tools

The path-taking ones take **run files or directories**, defaulting to this
directory, so a later sweep can be analyzed on its own or pooled with the
baseline. None of them calls the model.

The three that read only run files run under plain `node`. The two that reach
into the database — `ground-truth.mjs` and `show-prompt.mjs` — import the app's
TypeScript, so they need `npx tsx`; under `node` they fail with
`ERR_MODULE_NOT_FOUND`.

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| `analyze.mjs [paths…] [--out=file]` | Writes and prints `summary.md`: run manifest, rule-adherence counts, top-5s, rep stability, within- and between-cell ordering agreement, reason quality. **Start here** |
| `merge-runs.mjs [paths…] [--dry-run]` | Collapses many sweep files into one per snapshot and query, named `<anchor-date>-<query>.json`. Run it after a comparison, then `git rm` the pieces |
| `where-is.mjs <name> [paths…] [--cells=sub]` | One Option's rank and rationale in every run — the per-Option view the aggregates flatten. Grouped by snapshot |
| `ground-truth.mjs <name> [--log=N]` | What the **database** records about that Option: tags, notes, every Rejection with its date, when it was eaten. Needs `DATABASE_URL` |
| `show-prompt.mjs [query]` | The exact prompt a run sent. Writes `system-prompt.txt` (static half, committed) and `prompt-dump.txt` (full request, gitignored). Needs `DATABASE_URL` |
| `runs.mjs` | Shared loader — path expansion, snapshot identity, pooling helpers |

Use `ground-truth.mjs` before drawing any conclusion that rests on a household
constraint. A fluent rationale is indistinguishable from a datum until checked:
in this baseline every model's phrasing suggested Tadu has "no Thursday dinner
service", but the actual record is a single dated Rejection reading _"closed on
tues. only open for 5:30 dinner on sat, sun, mon."_ — the constraint is real,
the wording was the model's.

## Files

One run file per snapshot and query, named for the **anchor day** — the date
the models were ranking, which is also what decides whether two sweeps may be
pooled. (The baseline was executed on 2026-09-18 against a 2026-09-17 anchor;
the file is named for the latter.)

When a code change alters the snapshot or the prompt mid-day — same anchor
day and query — the runs after it cannot pool with the runs before. They go
in `<anchor-date>-<query>-<change>.json`, the suffix naming what changed.
`merge-runs.mjs` refuses a snapshot change on its own, but it cannot see a
prompt change, so never run it over two such files.

Each run carries cell, rep, model, thinking config, latency, output tokens, and
the full ranking; rows hold Option **names**, not UUIDs. Runs from 2026-09-22's
later sweeps also carry `rawText` — the model's response exactly as written,
before parsing — and `backfilledCount`, how many ranking rows `search` appended
because the model left them out (they sit at the bottom with the reason "Not
ranked — the AI left this one out"). `source` and
`sourceRep` record which original sweep a run arrived in, since both files are
merges of several — `mergedFrom` lists them. Three runs are `ok: false` (two in
the open file, one in the narrowing one), all of them `sonnet-5 · high` calls
that aborted at the production 90s timeout — kept because that abort *is*
finding 1. That is why `sonnet-5 · high` has the fewest usable reps of any cell
in both files, and why its rows in `summary.md` read as anecdote.

The originals — nine `--json=` files and their nine `.log` transcripts — were
merged away by `merge-runs.mjs`. The `.log` files were a pretty-printed view of
the same JSON and held nothing it does not; `git show 60175a8` has them all if
that ever proves wrong.

## What to look at

Ranking quality here is a judgment call, not a score — there is no ground truth
for "what should we eat tonight". `summary.md` counts only what the system
prompt states as explicit rules; everything else is a human read. Angles that
actually discriminate:

1. **Does the rationale name a pattern, or just restate recency?** The whole
   point of the AI path (ADR-0005) is to beat the deterministic recency sort in
   `lib/ranking.ts`. A rationale like "overdue after a long gap" is recency
   wearing a hat; "the standing Wednesday pick" is the feature working.
2. **Prompt adherence.** No Option name opening a rationale, no dates or
   day-counts ("9 days ago", "5/14"), one clause per rationale with nothing
   appended, and in `pithy` tail mode the rationale should *shrink* down the
   ranking — weak picks get a few words, obviously bad picks an empty reason.
   Note that weekday names are *encouraged*, not a break: the prompt's own good
   example is "the standing Wednesday pick".
3. **Top-5 agreement** across cells and across reps — and whether the
   disagreements are defensible or arbitrary.
4. **Whole-list ordering**, which is what the actual use paradigm reads:
   someone scanning from the top for inspiration goes well past five. Spearman
   and `mean move` in `summary.md` cover this; a tight top 5 and a stable list
   are different properties, and in this baseline they disagreed (findings 9
   and 12).
5. **Rationale/Option coherence** — does the reason actually describe the
   Option it is attached to? See finding 4.
6. **Cost/latency** against the table above.

## Running a later comparison

`.env` in this worktree carries `DATABASE_URL` + `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` (copied
from `.env.k8s` in the main checkout). Every cell is a live, billed API call —
the baseline above cost roughly 50 of them.

One sweep, all the cells being compared, written to a scratch file:

```
npx tsx scripts/ai-search-eval.ts --compare --serial --reps=3 \
  --cells="opus-5 · effort low,opus-5 · effort high,sonnet-5 · effort low" \
  --json=scripts/model-comparison/sweep-1.json
```

Top up reps, or re-run one cell past the timeout, into further scratch files
(`sweep-2.json`, …). Then collapse them and drop the pieces:

```
node scripts/model-comparison/merge-runs.mjs --dry-run   # check the grouping
node scripts/model-comparison/merge-runs.mjs             # -> <anchor-date>-<query>.json
git rm scripts/model-comparison/sweep-1.json ...
```

Merging is what keeps this directory from accumulating a file per sweep. It
pools only runs that share an anchor day *and* a query, refuses to merge two
sweeps whose Catalog or Log size differs under the same anchor day, and leaves
the analysis numbers untouched — verified on the baseline: after collapsing
nine files into two, every aggregate in `summary.md` came out byte-identical.

Between the merge and the `git rm`, the directory holds both the merged file and
its sources. Every tool here de-duplicates by origin (`source` + `sourceRep` +
cell), so that window reads the same as the tidied directory will — a run is
counted once, and the manifest in `summary.md` says which file the copies came
from. Re-running `merge-runs.mjs` in that state rewrites the same file rather
than a doubled one.

Then analyze — the whole directory, or one file:

```
node scripts/model-comparison/analyze.mjs
node scripts/model-comparison/analyze.mjs scripts/model-comparison/2026-09-17-open.json --out=/tmp/open.md
```

Five things to hold to, each learned the expensive way:

1. **All the cells being compared go in ONE invocation.** The harness builds
   one snapshot per run and sends it to every cell, so a difference between
   cells is the model. Two separate invocations are two snapshots, and on a
   different day they are not comparable at all.
2. **`--serial` for the output-token column.** Token counts are read from the
   `ai_search` log line, which can only be attributed to its own call when
   nothing else is in flight.
3. **A date rollover invalidates pooling.** `analyze.mjs` keys every pooled
   measure on snapshot identity (`today · options · log entries`) and reports
   what it excluded, so a rollover shows up rather than reading as model
   instability — but the reps are then simply lost. Check the date before
   launching a top-up sweep; that is cheaper than re-running it.
4. **Give a slow model room before judging it.** `AI_TIMEOUT_MS=300000`
   overrides the production 90s budget. Without it, a model that merely needs
   150s is indistinguishable from one that is broken: both collapse to
   `AI_SEARCH_UNAVAILABLE`. Record the honest latency, then judge it against
   90s separately — that is how finding 1 was reached.
5. **Diff the prompt.** `npx tsx scripts/model-comparison/show-prompt.mjs` writes
   `system-prompt.txt`; if it differs from the committed one, the prompt
   changed between sweeps and some of the findings above are about a prompt
   that no longer exists.

Sample-size discipline: at n=2 this comparison produced two confident findings
that did not survive n=5 (see the correction under finding 7). Three reps is
the floor for saying anything, and a between-cell difference means nothing
until it exceeds the cells' own rep-to-rep noise — which is why `summary.md`
prints that floor next to every pair (finding 12).
